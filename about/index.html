Beyond the Data

November 22, 2025

“I used to see the world in equations — now I’m drawn to the experiences behind them.”

👋 Hello, I’m Bai Xueyi.#

I am a Master of Data Science student at Monash University, preparing to enter the Research Stream in 2026.

My academic roots are in the abstract, logical world of Mathematics. For a long time, I found beauty in proofs and numbers. But as I engaged more with technology, I found myself increasingly curious about the intersection of data and people — how digital systems shape the way we feel, understand, and experience the world.

Today, I see my work motivation sitting between Data Science and Human–Computer Interaction (HCI).

🌟 What Motivates Me?#

My research interests are rooted not just in theory, but in lived experiences.

1. The Spark — Digital Immersion
Visiting TeamLab’s exhibitions was a turning point. It showed me that technology can be emotional and spatial, not just functional — that interaction can be felt as much as it is used.

2. The “Why”: Accessibility
My interest in accessibility comes from volunteering at the Shaanxi History Museum, where I assisted visitors with visual impairments.

I noticed that text descriptions often failed to convey shape and nuance. For instance, distinguishing between a Ding (鼎) and a Li (鬲). Both are three-legged bronze vessels, but the Li features unique hollow legs designed for heat efficiency.

A typical Ding (鼎) with solid, columnar legs.

A Li (鬲) with unique hollow, pouch-like legs designed for heat efficiency.

“To a screen reader, they might sound the same; but physically, they are distinct engineering marvels. This gap highlighted the limitations of text and inspired me to explore multisensory solutions.”

Recently, I’ve been exploring how sound, touch, and data might offer new ways for blind or low-vision visitors to experience visual art. More broadly, I’m curious about how sensory design can support cultural participation.

🌱 Current Curiosity#

These moments guide my current interests in inclusive, sensory-aware design.

I am particularly curious about how sound, touch, and data can offer alternative pathways for blind or low-vision visitors to experience visual art.
More broadly, I’m exploring how multisensory interaction can support cultural participation and create meaningful forms of engagement.

(I see this as an evolving direction — one I hope to explore more deeply through future research.)

🍂 Beyond Research#

Outside of coding and data pipelines, I’m drawn to slower sensory experiences:

  • 📷 Photography: Exploring digital and film photography.
  • 🧵 Making: Experimenting with small 3D-printed objects, simple woodworking projects, and hands-on craft. I enjoy learning how form, weight, and texture shift when ideas become physical.
  • ✒️ Calligraphy: Practising Chinese calligraphy as a quiet way to explore rhythm, balance, and gesture.
  • 🎧 Sound: Curating playlists for deep focus and evening walks.
  • 🏛 Observation: Wandering through galleries to observe how spaces tell stories.
  • 🏃 Movement: I enjoy distance running and practising gentle strength routines such as Jingang Gong (金刚功). These help me stay grounded, energetic, and aware of my body’s rhythm.

I enjoy noticing quiet details — light, sound, texture, scent, weight — and these often shape how I think about interaction, materiality, and experience.
